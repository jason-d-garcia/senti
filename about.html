senti is a mood map for music. Move the light to how you’re feeling, tap play, and it finds a song that fits. No sign-in and nothing to install.

How it works

The map has two directions. Left to right is how good a feeling is, from rough to great. Bottom to top is how much energy it has, from calm to wired. Every spot on the map is a feeling: tense and alert up top, serene and relaxed down below, happy on one side and sad on the other.

Each song sits somewhere on that map. Right now we place songs with a model that reads their lyrics and learned from songs that researchers had already labelled by mood. Next, we’re teaching it to listen to the music itself, not just read the words.

Why “senti”

Senti is Italian. It comes from sentire, which means both to feel and to hear. Italians also say “senti!” the way we’d say “listen!” or “hey, check this out.”

That’s the whole idea in one word: music is something you feel and hear at the same time, and the best way to share a song is to tell someone “listen to this.”

What we’re aiming for

Most music apps sort songs by genre, by artist, or by what you played last week. senti sorts them by feeling. The goal is a place where you can say exactly how you feel and get the right song back.

Today the map knows 16 feelings. Real moods are more specific than that: nostalgic, bittersweet, triumphant, late-night. We want senti to learn those finer shades, so you can zoom into “happy” and pick the exact kind of happy you mean.

How you can help

senti gets better the more people use it. The best things you can do:

  1. Use it. Play songs, explore the map, hit “discover more” and wander around.
  2. Rate songs. After a song plays, tell us whether it matched. If it didn’t, drop it where it belongs on the map. Those corrections are the most useful thing anyone can give us.
  3. Tag the vibe. Add a few words for how a song feels. Tags are how we’ll find the feelings the map doesn’t name yet.
  4. Share it. Send a friend a song with the share button. Every link opens straight onto that song.

Every rating and tag becomes training data. The more we collect, the better the model gets at placing songs, and the closer we get to more nuanced searches, like picking “wistful” instead of just “sad.”

Why there’s no Spotify login (yet)

We’d love to let you build playlists and find songs you already love by mood, right inside your Spotify. But Spotify only opens its full developer access to apps with around 250,000 monthly users. Until then, every song links out to Spotify and Apple Music instead.

So if you want that feature, the fastest way there is sharing senti with your friends.

Your privacy

There are no accounts. Ratings and tags are saved without your name or email, under a random ID that lives in your browser. Your network address is only used for a couple of minutes to stop spam, then thrown away.

Curious about the model, the data and the code? The technical details are on GitHub. Mood labels come from the MERGE dataset (CC BY-NC 4.0).
